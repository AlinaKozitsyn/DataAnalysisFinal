# Internet Addiction across Sex × Exercise Clusters

**Does psychological distress predict internet addiction differently across groups of adolescents?**

A data-analysis project on 819 Bangladeshi adolescents. We treat **perceived internet
addiction** (the Internet Addiction Test score) as the outcome of **depression** and
**loneliness**, inside four **sex × physical-activity** clusters, and then forecast the
**chance** that a student is internet-addicted.

> Course: CIS2601 — Introduction to Data Science (2025–2026)
> Authors: Noam Avizmil · Tomer Golshani · May Mandalaoui · Alina Kozitsyna

---

## Research questions

1. **Exploratory** — Within each of the four clusters (girls who exercise, girls who don't,
   boys who exercise, boys who don't), do **depression** and **loneliness** predict a higher
   **internet-addiction score** (`TotalIA`), and is that link stronger in some clusters?
2. **Forecast** — Given a student's distress and cluster, can we predict the **chance they are
   internet-addicted** (`TotalIA ≥ 31`, the Young cutoff)?

## Key findings

- **Depression drives internet addiction in every cluster** (standardized β ≈ 0.38–0.47, all
  *p* < .001; 25–34% of variance). **Loneliness adds almost nothing** once depression is in.
- **Exercise does *not* flatten the distress→addiction slope.** The cluster interaction is
  non-significant (F(6, 805) = 0.65, *p* = 0.69). What differs between clusters is **prevalence**
  rather than slope: about 45% of non-exercising boys score as addicted versus about 20% of girls.
- **Forecast:** logistic **AUC = 0.76** (accuracy 0.78 vs 0.74 baseline). Depression, being
  male, older age and being bullied all raise the odds of addiction.

## Data

The raw survey is **not committed**. Download it and place it at the path below.

- **Source:** Mahmud, A., Siddik, M. A. B., Arefin, M. M., & Rahman, M. M. (2024). *A data set
  exploring the link and associated factors of internet addiction, loneliness, and depression
  among adolescents in Bangladesh* [Data set]. **Mendeley Data, V2**,
  [doi:10.17632/yzcwfyrxy2.2](https://doi.org/10.17632/yzcwfyrxy2.2) (CC BY 4.0).
- **Place the Excel file at:** `data/New Microsoft Excel Worksheet.xlsx`
- 819 students · ages 13–19 · one row per student · **no missing values** · convenience sample
  (Sylhet, Bangladesh) collected via Google Forms behind an informed-consent gate.

| Role | Variable | Instrument |
|------|----------|------------|
| Addiction (outcome) | `TotalIA` (0–94), `IAC` band | Internet Addiction Test (Young, 1998) |
| Depression | `totalphq` (0–27) | PHQ-9 (Kroenke et al., 2001) |
| Loneliness | `lonelinesstotal` | UCLA Loneliness Scale v3 (Russell, 1996) |
| Clusters / controls | `gender`, `exercise`, `age`, `bullied` | self-report |

Forecast target: `addicted = 1` if `IAC ≥ 2` (`TotalIA ≥ 31`).

## Methods (brief)

- **4 linear regressions**, one per cluster: `TotalIA ~ totalphq + lonelinesstotal + age + bullied`.
- A **pooled interaction model** testing whether the distress slopes differ across clusters.
- A **logistic regression** forecasting `P(addicted)` from depression + loneliness + sex +
  exercise + age + bullied — 70/30 train/test split (fit on train, metrics on the held-out test;
  odds ratios reported on the full sample); ROC/AUC, confusion matrix, accuracy / sensitivity /
  specificity / F1.
- Assumptions checked **visually** (Residuals-vs-Fitted, Normal Q–Q) and via the simple
  predictor correlation, with nothing beyond the course syllabus.

> **Association, not causation.** All measures are self-reported on a single cross-sectional
> Google Form, so we report associations only and cannot establish direction.
